The clean transfer from ESDM is **not** to reproduce its entire wave-and-entropy machinery inside a Stable-Diffusion clone. It is to retain its central geometric contract: a frozen ArrowSpace feature Laplacian defines the valid low-energy semantic subspace, while a spatial latent retains image-specific local detail. This directly preserves your intended 2.5-D split: an item/spatial coordinate coupled to a feature-graph signal, with decoding adapted to the graph’s topology rather than an ambient sphere.

I would call the resulting model **Arrow Latent Diffusion with Spectral Chart Conditioning (ALD-SC)**.

## Architectural decision

Use **one corpus-level, frozen** ArrowSpace geometry—not a newly constructed graph per image.

```math
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Arrow}}
=
\left(
L_F,\;
U_q,\;
\Lambda_q,\;
\lambda^{\mathrm{ED}}
\right)
```

where:

- $$L_F \in \mathbb{R}^{F \times F}$$ is the feature-space graph Laplacian built once from the training corpus;
- $$U_q \in \mathbb{R}^{F \times q}$$ contains the $$q$$ retained smooth eigenvectors;
- $$\Lambda_q = \mathop{\text{diag}}(\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_q)$$ contains the corresponding Laplacian eigenvalues;
- $$\lambda^{\mathrm{ED}} \in [0,1]^F$$ is the ArrowSpace energy-dispersion distribution over feature nodes.

This is the most important ESDM lesson to preserve: the graph is a **geometric prior**, not a flexible learned adjacency that can drift into a convenient but semantically meaningless representation. ESDM’s frozen $L_F,U_q$ separation between structural geometry and learned dynamics is exactly the right simplification for this setting.

## The 2.5-D latent

Encode an image into two coupled objects:

```math
x
\;\xrightarrow{E_{\phi}}\;
\left(z,\; A\right),
```

with

```math
z \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w \times c},
\qquad
A \in \mathbb{R}^{hw \times F}.
```

- $$z$$ is the ordinary spatial image latent used by the diffusion model.
- $$A$$ is an $$F$$-channel semantic feature field, flattened over the $h \times w$ latent plane.

The ArrowSpace projection operates over the **feature axis**, exactly as in ESDM:

```math
\Pi_q = U_q U_q^\top,
\qquad
A_{\parallel} = A\Pi_q,
\qquad
A_{\perp} = A(I-\Pi_q).
```

Then pool the smooth feature field into a compact spectral chart:

```math
s
=
\mathop{\text{Pool}}(A_{\parallel}) U_q
\in \mathbb{R}^{q}.
```

The complete representation is therefore:

```math
\boxed{
\mathcal{Z}_{2.5D}(x)
=
\left(
z,\;
s,\;
e
\right)
}
```

where $$e$$ is a small vector of spectral-band energies. The spatial latent gives the model its “2D”; the ArrowSpace spectral chart gives it the topology-adaptive “half dimension.” This keeps the intended representation simple: a conventional latent plane plus a low-dimensional graph-manifold coordinate system.

## Spectral energy descriptor

Avoid sending the full $$F \times F$$ Laplacian to the diffusion backbone. It is fixed anyway, so represent each image through its energy allocation over the frozen graph modes.

For the $$k$$-th spectral mode, define:

```math
e_k
=
\frac{1}{hw}
\left\|
A u_k
\right\|_2^2,
\qquad
k = 1,\ldots,q.
```

This says how much of the image’s feature activation lies in each ArrowSpace mode. Form a normalized band descriptor:

```math
\tilde e_k
=
\frac{e_k}{\sum_{j=1}^{q} e_j + \varepsilon}.
```

Then combine it with the corpus energy-dispersion prior projected onto the chart:

```math
\lambda^{(k)}_{\mathrm{chart}}
=
\sum_{f=1}^{F}
\lambda^{\mathrm{ED}}_f U_{f,k}^2.
```

The conditioning state becomes:

```math
c_{\mathrm{spec}}
=
\mathop{\text{MLP}}
\left(
[
\tilde e_1,\ldots,\tilde e_q,\;
\lambda^{(1)}_{\mathrm{chart}},\ldots,
\lambda^{(q)}_{\mathrm{chart}},\;
\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_q
]
\right).
```

This retains the ESDM distinction between:

- **Laplacian structure:** where smooth and rough semantic directions lie;
- **energy dispersion:** where real corpus samples tend to lie on that structure.

The two should remain separate inputs, rather than treating lambdas as an arbitrary post-hoc weighting of the graph.

## Simple model diagram

```text
                         Frozen ArrowSpace prior
              ┌────────────────────────────────────┐
              │ L_F, U_q, Λ_q, λ_ED                │
              └────────────────────────────────────┘
                              │
image x ── Encoder ──► spatial latent z ──► Latent DiT ──► ẑ
              │                    │                     │
              ▼                    │                     ▼
        feature field A             └──── spectral tokens ─┐
              │                                             │
              ▼                                             ▼
   project: A U_q U_q^T                          topology-conditioned
              │                                  VAE decoder
              ▼                                             │
   chart coordinates + band energy                           ▼
         s, e ──► c_spec ───────────────────────────────► image x̂
```

The only new components relative to a standard latent diffusion system are:

1. A feature-field output head in the encoder.
2. A fixed ArrowSpace projection.
3. A compact spectral conditioner.
4. Spectral gates in the decoder.

The denoiser itself can remain an otherwise ordinary DiT or U-Net.

## Encoder

Use a VAE-style encoder with a shared trunk and two heads:

```math
(z,\;A) = E_{\phi}(x).
```

### Spatial head

```math
z = E_z(x)
\in \mathbb{R}^{h \times w \times c}.
```

This is the standard latent-diffusion state. For a 512 by 512 prototype, $z$ could be $64 \times 64 \times 4$ or $32 \times 32 \times 8$.

### Feature head

```math
A = E_A(x)
\in \mathbb{R}^{hw \times F}.
```

Choose $$F$$ to match the ArrowSpace index dimensionality—for example, 128 or 256 channels. The feature head should branch from an intermediate encoder scale, not from final RGB reconstruction features, because it must represent semantic fields rather than merely texture.

Apply the ESDM projection without a new trainable graph:

```math
A_{\parallel} = A U_qU_q^\top.
```

Optionally keep a small residual channel:

```math
A_{\mathrm{enc}}
=
A_{\parallel}
+
\beta A_{\perp},
\qquad
0 \leq \beta \leq 0.1.
```

Start with $\beta=0$. This enforces the same practical goal as ESDM’s restriction to dominant smooth modes: generation should begin in the meaningful spectral subspace rather than learn to repair arbitrary off-manifold feature noise.

## Diffusion backbone

The diffusion process acts only on $$z$$, not on the $$F \times F$$ graph and not directly on the spectral coefficients:

```math
q(z_t \mid z_0)
=
\mathcal{N}
\left(
\sqrt{\bar \alpha_t}z_0,
(1-\bar \alpha_t)I
\right).
```

Train a denoiser:

```math
v_{\theta}
\left(
z_t,\;
t,\;
c_{\mathrm{text}},\;
c_{\mathrm{spec}}
\right).
```

Use $v$-prediction or flow matching. The spectral state is injected as cross-attention tokens or AdaLN conditioning:

```math
H_{\ell+1}
=
\mathop{\text{DiTBlock}}
\left(
H_{\ell};
\gamma_{\ell}(c_{\mathrm{spec}}),
\beta_{\ell}(c_{\mathrm{spec}})
\right).
```

This is deliberately simpler than pushing a wave recurrence through every diffusion step. The geometry governs the **latent prior and decoder**, while the DiT learns the image distribution in the usual robust manner.

### Why not diffuse s separately?

Do not initially run a second diffusion process over $s$. It introduces a coupled reverse process, schedule synchronization, and consistency failure modes.

Instead:

- During training, obtain $$c_{\mathrm{spec}}$$ from the encoded image.
- During text-to-image generation, predict $$c_{\mathrm{spec}}$$ from the prompt using a small prior network, or draw it from an empirical conditional distribution.
- During image-to-image or editing, retain or interpolate the source image’s spectral chart.

A later version can jointly diffuse $$z$$ and $$s$$ if the empirical prior proves too weak.

## Topology decoder

The decoder should be conditioned by the spectral chart, but it should **not** attempt a literal matrix inverse of $\mathcal H$. The ESDM language of “inverse semantic operator” is conceptually useful, but $U_qU_q^\top$ is rank-deficient when $q<F$, and heat diffusion is non-invertible without unstable amplification of high-frequency modes.

Use a stable constructive inverse instead:

```math
\hat x
=
D_{\psi}
\left(
\hat z,\;
c_{\mathrm{spec}}
\right).
```

At each upsampling level, form a feature field $H_\ell \in \mathbb{R}^{n_\ell \times F}$, project it onto the ArrowSpace chart, and gate its spectral bands:

```math
\hat H_\ell
=
H_\ell U_q
\in \mathbb{R}^{n_\ell \times q},
```

```math
g_\ell
=
\sigma
\left(
W_\ell c_{\mathrm{spec}} + b_\ell
\right)
\in \mathbb{R}^{q},
```

```math
H_{\ell+1}
=
\mathop{\text{UpBlock}}_{\ell}
\left(
H_\ell
+
(\hat H_\ell \odot g_\ell)U_q^\top
\right).
```

This provides a clean topology-adaptive decoder:

- $$U_q$$ determines the permitted smooth feature directions.
- $$c_{\mathrm{spec}}$$ determines how strongly each direction is expressed for this particular image.
- The spatial decoder determines where those modes appear on the image plane.

The graph topology thus controls *semantic energy allocation*, while convolutional or attention-based decoding still handles local geometry, boundaries, and texture.

## Heat-kernel prior

Reuse ESDM’s heat-kernel idea as a prior over chart coefficients, but avoid coupling it to the full entropy-clock machinery initially.

Let

```math
s = [s_1,\ldots,s_q]^\top.
```

Use:

```math
p_t(s)
=
\mathcal{N}
\left(
0,\;
\mathop{\text{diag}}
\left[
w_k e^{-2t\nu_k}
\right]_{k=1}^{q}
\right),
```

where $w_k$ derives from the projected ArrowSpace energy-dispersion prior:

```math
w_k
=
\frac{
\exp(-\rho \lambda^{(k)}_{\mathrm{chart}})
}{
\sum_j
\exp(-\rho \lambda^{(j)}_{\mathrm{chart}})
}.
```

Interpretation:

- Lower Laplacian eigenvalues retain more variance under heat flow.
- Modes preferred by the corpus energy distribution receive more prior mass.
- Sampling stays concentrated in semantic basins without asserting that all image content is reconstructible from only $q$ spectral coordinates.

The spatial latent $$z$$ carries the residual visual degrees of freedom; $$s$$ provides a low-dimensional, manifold-aware control state.

## What to retain

| ESDM concept | ALD-SC implementation |
| :-- | :-- |
| Frozen $$L_F,U_q$$ geometric prior | Build ArrowSpace once on the image-feature corpus; store as model buffers |
| Bright/dark dual-space split | Bright: spatial latent $$z$$; dark: frozen feature graph and chart prior |
| Projection $$\Phi(Q)=QU_qU_q^\top$$ | Apply $$A \mapsto AU_qU_q^\top$$ to encoder/decoder feature fields |
| Low-mode spectral chart | Use coefficients $$s$$ and band energies $$e$$ as compact graph conditioning |
| Heat-kernel manifold | Put a diagonal heat-kernel prior on spectral chart coordinates |
| ArrowSpace lambdas | Use as empirical weights over chart modes, distinct from Laplacian eigenvalues |
| Topology-adaptive decoding | Spectrally gated decoder blocks using the fixed $$U_q$$ basis |

This retains the parts of ESDM that make a sharp architectural claim: the graph defines the intrinsic generative geometry, and the decoder is conditioned by that geometry rather than assuming the ambient latent space is spherical.

## What to defer

| ESDM concept | Why defer it in v1 |
| :-- | :-- |
| Full second-order wave recurrence | Adds stability conditions, a previous-state dependency, and a second dynamics model before proving the graph-conditioned latent is useful |
| Rayleigh-gradient restoring force | Useful as a regularizer or ablation, but not required for the first decoder |
| Bright/dark KL entropy clock | Difficult to align with standard inference-time diffusion schedules; its benefit should be separately demonstrated |
| Learned pump $\Lambda(\tau) B_t$ | Risks reintroducing the force-balance tuning problem ESDM was designed to control |
| Per-sample dynamic Laplacian | Confounds the frozen semantic prior with image-specific geometry and weakens the shared manifold claim |

This is consistent with your preference for the tractable tau-mode-style approximation over the full exact Rayleigh construction when the latter makes the mechanism expensive or difficult to control.

## Training losses

Use a conventional latent-diffusion objective plus only three spectral losses:

```math
\mathcal L
=
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{diff}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{rec}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rec}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{chart}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{chart}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{smooth}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{smooth}}.
```

### Latent diffusion

```math
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{diff}}
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\left\|
v - v_\theta(z_t,t,c_{\mathrm{text}},c_{\mathrm{spec}})
\right\|_2^2
\right].
```

### Reconstruction

```math
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rec}}
=
\|x-\hat x\|_1
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{perc}}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{LPIPS}}(x,\hat x).
```

### Spectral chart consistency

Re-encode the decoded image and preserve its spectral energy allocation:

```math
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{chart}}
=
\left\|
\tilde e(x)
-
\tilde e(\hat x)
\right\|_2^2.
```

### Off-manifold penalty

Penalize feature energy outside the smooth ArrowSpace subspace:

```math
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{smooth}}
=
\frac{
\|A(I-U_qU_q^\top)\|_F^2
}{
\|A\|_F^2 + \varepsilon
}.
```

This loss operationalizes the original ESDM goal—off-manifold generation should be difficult—without requiring a full vibrational recurrence.

## Minimal experiment

Start with a controlled image-domain experiment:

- Use a pretrained DINO, SigLIP, or encoder feature corpus to build the frozen ArrowSpace $$L_F$$.
- Set $$F=256$$, $$q=16$$ or $$32$$.
- Train a small VAE with the spectral conditioner but **without diffusion**.
- Compare a normal VAE against the topology-conditioned VAE at equal latent capacity.
- Only after demonstrating improved reconstruction or better latent interpolation, train the DiT denoiser.

Measure:

- Pixel reconstruction: PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS.
- Spectral preservation: chart-energy error and off-manifold energy ratio.
- Generative quality: FID or domain-appropriate alternatives.
- Structural consistency: agreement of $$AU_qU_q^\top$$ between real and generated images.
- Controllability: interpolate $$s$$ while holding $$z$$ fixed, then inspect whether global semantic organization changes smoothly.

The core falsifiable claim is:

```math
\text{A frozen ArrowSpace spectral chart improves latent-image decoding and control at fixed } z \text{ capacity.}
```

If that claim holds, ESDM’s entropy clock and vibrational force can become well-isolated second-stage contributions rather than entangled prerequisites.
