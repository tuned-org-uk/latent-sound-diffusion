let's design an architecture that:
1. encode the pixel-space into a latent representation that is guided by the graph Laplacian and energy dispersion lambdas in feature-space as in https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21535
2. define a decoder to take advantage of the compression provided by arrowspace (geometric 3D to 2.5D FxF graph Laplacian and lambdas sequence)
outline a simil-stable diffusion model

A **spectrally conditioned latent-diffusion autoencoder**, where image latents retain local spatial content while ArrowSpace supplies a compact *feature-topology* state: an $$F \times F$$ Laplacian plus a truncated energy-dispersion spectrum. This maps well onto your paper’s framing of embedding columns as nodes in a feature-space energy network, rather than treating embeddings only as pointwise vectors. [1]

## Proposed model: Arrow-LDM

Let an image be $$x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$$. The model has four trainable components:

$$
x
\;\xrightarrow{E_{\phi}}\;
(z,\; A)
\;\xrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}\;
(L_F,\Lambda_F,\mathcal{C})
\;\xrightarrow{\text{diffusion}}\;
\hat z
\;\xrightarrow{D_{\psi}(\cdot \mid \hat L_F,\hat\Lambda_F,\mathcal{C})}\;
\hat x
$$

- $$E_{\phi}$$: image encoder producing a standard spatial latent $$z$$ and an intermediate feature field $$A$$.
- $$\mathcal{W}$$: ArrowSpace wiring operator over **feature columns** of $$A$$.
- Spectral conditioner: turns the feature Laplacian and energy-dispersion spectrum into compact conditioning tokens.
- $$D_{\psi}$$: spectrally conditioned decoder that reconstructs pixels, while the diffusion model generates or denoises $$z$$.

The key distinction from Stable Diffusion is that its VAE latent primarily compresses image content, whereas Arrow-LDM explicitly preserves a low-dimensional description of the image’s **feature-energy topology**. The stated ArrowSpace/Graph Wiring premise is precisely that feature-space topology carries spectral information complementary to geometric similarity. [1]

## Encoder and wiring

Use a convolutional or ViT-style encoder that produces:

$$
A = E_{\phi}^{\mathrm{feat}}(x)
\in \mathbb{R}^{N \times F},
\qquad N = h \cdot w
$$

where each row is a spatial location/patch and each column $$a_f \in \mathbb{R}^{N}$$ is a feature activation field over the image.

This makes the ArrowSpace graph naturally **feature-centric**:

$$
G_F = (V_F, W_F), \qquad |V_F| = F
$$

with edge weights derived from relationships between feature columns. A practical starting point is:

$$
W_{ij}
=
\exp\left(
-\frac{d(a_i,a_j)^2}{\tau}
\right)
\cdot m_{ij},
$$

where $$d$$ may be cosine, correlation, or a learned symmetric distance, $$m_{ij}$$ is a sparse $$k$$-NN mask, and $$\tau$$ is your ArrowSpace-style temperature/modulation parameter. This reflects the paper’s use of feature-column topology and adaptive $$\tau$$-modulation in the spectral search setting. [1]

Build a normalized graph Laplacian:

$$
L_F = I - D^{-1/2} W D^{-1/2},
\qquad
L_F = U \Lambda U^\top.
$$

Here, $$\Lambda = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_F)$$ is the **energy-dispersion sequence**. Low eigenvalues encode smooth, globally coherent feature modes; higher eigenvalues encode increasingly oscillatory or locally varying feature relations.

## 3D to 2.5D representation

Your “3D to 2.5D” reduction can be implemented by separating local spatial geometry from global feature topology.

| Component | Full form | Compressed form | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pixel geometry | $$h \times w \times c$$ spatial feature tensor | Spatial latent $$z \in \mathbb{R}^{h' \times w' \times c_z}$$ | Local objects, layout, texture, edges |
| Feature geometry | $$A \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times F}$$ | $$F \times F$$ graph $$L_F$$ | Feature co-activation / exclusion structure |
| Spectral geometry | Eigenvector matrix $$U \in \mathbb{R}^{F \times F}$$ | Top-$$K$$ spectrum $$\Lambda_K$$, spectral moments, learned graph tokens | Global energy allocation and semantic coherence |
| Decoder control | Full feature field | $$K$$ spectral tokens plus spatial latent | Reinflate detail under topology constraints |

The “2.5D” object is therefore not simply a depth map. It is a **spatial latent plane plus a compact spectral side-channel**:

$$
\mathcal{Z}_{2.5D}
=
\left[
z,\;
\Lambda_K,\;
s_{\mathrm{ED}},\;
c_{\mathrm{graph}}
\right].
$$

A minimal spectral descriptor could be:

$$
s_{\mathrm{ED}}
=
\left[
\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_K,\;
\operatorname{tr}(L_F),\;
\operatorname{tr}(L_F^2),\;
\mathcal{E}_1,\ldots,\mathcal{E}_B
\right],
$$

where $$\mathcal{E}_b$$ is the activation energy assigned to a spectral band. This is more stable than making the decoder depend directly on all eigenvectors, which are ambiguous up to sign and become non-unique in repeated eigenspaces.

## Spectral latent tokens

Do not feed raw $$L_F$$ or raw eigenvectors directly into a U-Net. Convert the graph into a fixed number of permutation-invariant spectral tokens:

$$
q_k
=
\operatorname{Pool}_{f=1}^{F}
\left[
U_{fk},
\lambda_k,
\langle a_f,\, \Pi_k A\rangle,
d_f
\right],
\qquad k=1,\ldots,K,
$$

where:

- $$\Pi_k = u_k u_k^\top$$ is the sign-invariant rank-one spectral projector;
- $$d_f$$ is a degree or feature-energy statistic;
- $$q_k$$ becomes one learned conditioning token per retained band.

In practice, a safer and cheaper alternative is to avoid explicit eigendecomposition in the main path and use a bank of learned polynomial graph filters:

$$
g_b(L_F)A
=
\sum_{r=0}^{R} \alpha_{br} T_r(\widetilde L_F) A,
$$

with Chebyshev polynomials $$T_r$$. You can still compute the $$\lambda$$-sequence for supervision, diagnostics, and an auxiliary energy-dispersion loss. This preserves the intended spectral behavior while avoiding instability around degenerate eigenvalues.

## Diffusion backbone

Use a DiT-like latent denoiser rather than a classic spatial U-Net, because it gives you a clean place to inject both text and graph-spectrum tokens:

$$
\epsilon_{\theta}
\left(
z_t,\,
t,\,
c_{\text{text}},\,
q_{1:K}
\right).
$$

The forward process remains conventional latent diffusion:

$$
q(z_t \mid z_0)
=
\mathcal N
\left(
\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}z_0,\,
(1-\bar\alpha_t)I
\right).
$$

The reverse model predicts noise, velocity, or clean latent; I would begin with $$v$$-prediction for numerical stability:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{diff}}
=
\mathbb{E}_{z_0,\epsilon,t}
\left[
\left\|
v -
v_{\theta}(z_t,t,c_{\text{text}},q_{1:K})
\right\|_2^2
\right].
$$

The graph conditioning can enter each transformer block through:

- Cross-attention from spatial latent tokens to spectral tokens.
- AdaLN / FiLM modulation from a pooled spectral summary.
- A spectral-attention bias between latent patches, derived from graph-filtered feature affinities.
- Classifier-free guidance dropout independently on text and graph conditions, enabling text-only, graph-only, or joint generation.

The resulting generative model remains recognisably Stable-Diffusion-like: an autoencoder plus an iterative denoiser in a compressed continuous latent space. Latent diffusion was introduced to move the expensive iterative process out of full pixel space while retaining high-resolution synthesis capability. [2]

## Spectral decoder

The decoder should not merely decode $$z$$; it should **reconstruct pixels subject to a predicted or encoded feature-energy layout**:

$$
\hat x
=
D_{\psi}
\left(
z_0;\,
q_{1:K},\,
\hat L_F,\,
\hat\Lambda_K
\right).
$$

A strong decoder block is:

$$
H^{(\ell+1)}
=
\operatorname{ResBlock}
\left(
H^{(\ell)}
+
\gamma^{(\ell)}(q) \odot
\operatorname{GraphFilter}_{\hat L_F}
\left(H^{(\ell)}\right)
\right).
$$

Operationally:

1. Upsample the spatial latent as in a VAE decoder.
2. Project decoder channels into $$F$$ ArrowSpace feature channels.
3. Apply low-, middle-, and high-band filters associated with the $$\lambda$$-sequence.
4. Use spectral-token-conditioned gates to determine how much energy each band contributes.
5. Project the fused feature field back to RGB.

This means the decoder can assign:

- Low-$$\lambda$$ bands to global illumination, object grouping, composition, and broad semantic regions.
- Mid-spectrum bands to shape, part boundaries, and structural texture.
- High-$$\lambda$$ bands to fine texture, text-like detail, hair, foliage, and sharp local transitions.

The decoder thus uses ArrowSpace compression as a **global spectral scaffold**, while the spatial latent still carries the information that cannot be safely reduced to an $$F \times F$$ feature graph.

## Training objective

Train in stages initially; joint end-to-end training can come once the autoencoder is stable:

$$
\mathcal L
=
\lambda_{\mathrm{rec}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rec}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{perc}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{perc}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{diff}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{diff}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{spec}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{spec}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{energy}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{energy}}
+
\lambda_{\mathrm{graph}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{graph}}.
$$

Recommended terms:

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rec}}
=
\|x-\hat x\|_1
$$

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{spec}}
=
\sum_{k=1}^{K}
w_k
\left(
\lambda_k(L_F) -
\lambda_k(\hat L_F)
\right)^2
$$

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{energy}}
=
\sum_{b=1}^{B}
\left(
\mathcal E_b(A,L_F)-
\mathcal E_b(\hat A,\hat L_F)
\right)^2
$$

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{graph}}
=
\left\|
\operatorname{norm}(W_F)
-
\operatorname{norm}(\hat W_F)
\right\|_1.
$$

The reconstruction terms prevent the spectral bottleneck from becoming decorative. The spectrum and energy losses ensure that the decoded image reconstitutes the same feature-space topology and band allocation as the encoded image.

A spectrum-only loss is insufficient: non-isomorphic graphs can be cospectral. You therefore need either graph-affinity supervision, projector/subspace supervision, or filtered-signal consistency in addition to eigenvalue matching.

## Conditional generation modes

This architecture gives you several unusually useful modes:

| Mode | Inputs | Output use |
|---|---|---|
| Text-to-image | Text prompt | Standard generative imaging, graph prior sampled or predicted |
| Image-to-image | Source image, prompt | Preserve its spectral feature organization while changing semantics |
| Spectral style transfer | Content latent plus donor $$\Lambda_K$$/energy profile | Transfer global feature-energy allocation without direct pixel copying |
| Retrieval-guided generation | Query embedding, ArrowSpace graph state, prompt | Generate images aligned to a retrieved semantic-energy neighbourhood |
| Graph editing | Edit spectral-band energies or selected graph edges | Coherent global-to-local visual edits |
| Inversion and analysis | Image only | Explain an image through feature connectivity and spectral-energy allocation |

The retrieval-guided version is particularly aligned with your Energy Dispersion Networks framing: use a retrieved ArrowSpace neighbourhood to create a prior over $$\Lambda_K$$, graph-token statistics, and perhaps a selected set of graph modes. The paper explicitly motivates spectral structure as a complement to geometric similarity for semantic alignment and head-tail coherence. [1]

## Training plan

### Phase 1: Spectral VAE

Train the encoder, wiring module, and decoder without diffusion.

- Input: $$x$$.
- Output: $$z$$, $$L_F$$, $$\Lambda_K$$, graph tokens, reconstruction $$\hat x$$.
- Validate: reconstruction quality, spectral-band energy retention, and stability of graph descriptors under benign image augmentations.

Do not compress to only $$\Lambda_K$$ at this point. Use $$z$$ plus spectral tokens; the eigenvalue sequence is a global descriptor, not a sufficient statistic for reconstructing an image.

### Phase 2: Latent diffusion

Freeze most of the autoencoder and train the DiT/U-Net on $$z$$, conditioned on text and encoder-derived spectral tokens.

- Train graph-conditioned and graph-dropped cases.
- Predict the clean latent and optionally a clean spectral summary jointly.
- Measure whether graph conditioning improves long-range coherence, compositional consistency, and high-frequency reconstruction.

### Phase 3: Joint spectral consistency

Unfreeze the decoder adapters and fine-tune end-to-end with a low weight on the graph and energy losses.

- Apply spectral consistency to decoded images.
- Penalize excessive drift in low-frequency eigenspaces.
- Use a separate high-band loss sparingly; otherwise the model may learn to inject visually plausible but semantically unsupported texture.

## Minimum viable prototype

For a first experiment, keep the graph deliberately small and stable:

- Start with $$512 \times 512$$ images.
- Use a VAE latent $$z \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times 64 \times 4}$$, analogous to conventional latent-diffusion compression. [2]
- Extract a feature map $$A \in \mathbb{R}^{32 \times 32 \times 128}$$, giving $$N=1024$$, $$F=128$$.
- Construct a sparse $$k=8$$ feature graph over the 128 feature columns.
- Keep $$K=16$$ to 32 low spectral modes plus 8 band-energy scalars.
- Feed 24 to 40 graph tokens into a moderate DiT.
- Decode with FiLM-conditioned graph-filter residual blocks at the $$16^2$$, $$32^2$$, and $$64^2$$ feature resolutions.

That is compact enough to test the central hypothesis: **does a feature-graph spectrum provide generative control and reconstruction fidelity beyond a normal image VAE latent?**

## Main research hypothesis

The paper version could state the contribution as:

> We introduce Arrow-LDM, a spectrally conditioned latent diffusion architecture in which a pixel encoder induces an ArrowSpace graph over feature fields. A compact Laplacian-spectrum and energy-dispersion representation conditions both latent denoising and image decoding, preserving global feature topology while retaining a spatial latent for local reconstruction.

The scientific claim worth testing is not merely that spectral conditioning improves FID. It is that conditioning on feature-graph energy dispersion yields better **global semantic coherence under compression**, particularly under low latent capacity, out-of-distribution composition, retrieval augmentation, and controllable coarse-to-fine editing.
